The tick that collapsed

One saved world dragged a dedicated physics server from its nominal 64 ticks per second down to 10–17: about 50 ms wasted on every tick, for everyone connected. Fresh worlds ran full speed. The cause was one leftover record in the saved state, proven three independent ways.

Stormworks steps a whole physics simulation forward at a nominal 64 ticks per second, a fixed budget of a little under 16 ms per tick shared by everyone connected. One saved world didn't respect it: loaded on a dedicated server it ran at 10–17 TPS, a quarter to a sixth of nominal, the whole world stepping in slow motion for every player connected.

tbudget = 1000 ms / 64 ticks ≈ 15.6 ms per tick

10–17→~61TPS · ~99% recovered against a 64 nominal ceiling

Fresh vs loaded

A freshly spawned world (same engine, same physics library, same server binary) ran at the full 64 TPS. Only this one saved world crawled. That single contrast places the cost in the saved state a fresh world doesn't carry, not in the build.

The cost

Against the ~16 ms budget, the crawling world spent roughly 50 ms of extra time on every tick, paid by the server and so by everyone at once. And it recurred on every single tick, indefinitely: a cost that never amortizes is a cost being re-done from scratch each frame.

Root cause

A single stale persisted physics-contact record (the kind the physics library normally produces, consumes, and retires within a frame) had been persisted into the save and was re-cascaded every tick. A fresh world doesn't carry it, which is why fresh worlds ran at 64 TPS; this one did, and paid the tax on every tick.

Method withheld

This shows the symptom, the impact, the diagnostic reasoning, and the measured before-and-after, but not the method. How the offending record was located inside a stripped native binary and how it was cleared, the addresses, the probe technique, the dispatch path, and exactly what the fix touches stay private. The measurements are the proof; they are not a recipe.

Proof

One before/after proves nothing about cause. I confirmed it by repetition: three independent interventions, each attacking the same diagnosed record by a different route, each measured before and after on the running dedicated server.

The pattern

A single bad record in persisted state can tax every cycle of a system forever. It doesn't crash, error, or grow; it just sits there being reprocessed. Save games, session stores, replication logs, cache snapshots: anything that persists and reloads working state can carry one.

Fresh-versus-loaded isolates it: when a fresh instance is fast and a loaded one slow on the identical binary, the cost is in the state, not the logic. Independent reproduction closes it: three separate interventions converging on the same recovery is what turns "it got faster" into "this record was the cause."
