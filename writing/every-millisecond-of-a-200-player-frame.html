Over the Top: WWI puts up to 200 players in one battle. It is a Unity title compiled through IL2CPP, running D3D11 on D3D12: closed binary, no source, no symbols. Across a session it averaged about 9.3 ms a frame, roughly 108 fps. By that number there was nothing to fix. It still stuttered.

5–20→60+fps · p99.9 low

The tail, not the average

An average tells you what a typical frame costs, not what the worst one costs, and the worst frame is the one a player feels. In one session, 557 frames ran slower than 20 ms; the deepest ran 216 to 672 ms, a freeze of up to two-thirds of a second in a session whose mean looked healthy.

Ten worst frames · one sessionwhole-frame ms
The average frame was fine. These ten ran 20–75× over budget: the freezes players reported. Diagnosed, fixed, and shipped in Hotfix 1.0117.

No shipped tool measures the inside of this binary. Before touching a single dip, I needed to know where a frame's time goes.

A full frame budget

I built one from scratch: an inclusive-timing profiler that attributes the whole frame, with every millisecond assigned to the engine phase that spent it. It resolved the frame into 107 phases with 0.0 ms unaccounted. When a phase is expensive, there is nowhere for the cost to hide.

The fixes

What was wrongThe changeMeasured result
A per-frame update ran a 320-line state machine every single frame, ~99% of the time with nothing to do Skip the redundant work when there is no state change ~3.96 ms/frame saved · 2,255 calls, 99% skipped
A culling pass cost time on the heaviest frames Tighten the pass −6 ms at p99.9 · −1.7 ms average
The IK system ran at full fidelity for every player regardless of distance or visibility A level-of-detail gate on inverse-kinematics work 96.5 → 101 fps benchmarked · 73% of IK work skipped
Distant players were drawn at full cost Draw distant players as impostors +10–13 fps
UI update code churned every frame Remove the redundant update churn ~0.2 ms

The average barely moved; it was already fine. What changed is the tail: the frames that used to freeze now stay inside a playable band.

It shipped

The findings went to the small team as drop-in fixes with the before/after for each. The public patch notes for Hotfix 1.0117 list the same subsystems, in the studio's own words:

The pattern

Worst-case frames, not averages, are what users feel. Any system with a latency budget (a render loop, a request handler, a UI thread) is judged by its tail. An average can sit on top of a p99.9 an order of magnitude worse and hide it; optimize the mean and you optimize the thing nobody was complaining about.

And you can't fix what you can't measure. An inclusive budget that closes to zero, every phase accounted for and nothing unexplained, is what turns "it stutters sometimes" into a ranked list of specific, drop-in fixes. Build the ledger first; the expensive work names itself.
