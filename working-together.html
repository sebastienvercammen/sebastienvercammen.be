How an engagement runs

01

You describe the problem

A short written description of what's wrong and what you've already tried. I work from the public build your players downloaded, so there's nothing to set up on your side. If it's not a fit, I say so here, at no cost to either of us.

02

We agree on scope

One problem, picked together on a free call before anything is paid. You pick by pain, whichever one is costing you most, and we work out together what that actually covers. The first engagement is €2,500 fixed.

03

I deliver and prove it

A root-cause report inside two weeks: what is causing the defect, the evidence for it, and what it takes to fix it. You see progress in writing throughout, and a before/after anyone can verify on any work that follows.

The report arrives either way

If there is a cause to find, you get it: the cause, the evidence chain that proves it, and what it takes to fix it. If there is no cause to find, you get a report of what was done, what was ruled out, how it was measured, and the next steps to take. Same price, same deadline, and if it's beyond my abilities to diagnose it I'll say so plainly and explain why.

What you get

  • A fixed price, agreed before I start€2,500 for the first engagement. Anything after it is scoped the same way and quoted as a fixed fee, with milestones and a payment percentage attached to each.
  • A defined deliverableA root-cause report: the cause, the evidence, what it takes to fix it, and roughly what that fix costs. Written so an engineer on your team can start the implementation without me.
  • Written progress throughoutAsync and written-first. A halfway update at day seven, the report at day fourteen. You can see where the work stands without scheduling a meeting for it.

What I don't do

  • Hourly workNo day rates, no hourly billing. I take one defined problem and see it through to a proven result.
  • Open-ended engagementsEvery engagement is one problem, one price and one deadline, all three settled before it starts.

Where it starts

One problem, €2,500, and a root-cause analysis report inside two weeks. All you hand over is the public build, the same download your players have. No source code, no repo access, no engineer's time required to get started.

If the symptom turns out to have more than one cause, you get all of them. The two-week deadline is what bounds the engagement, not the number of causes.

The work behind the terms

Every one diagnosed from outside the studio, on a retail build, with no source access. Each row jumps to that case on the home page.

◆  4.6 min → 20 s
Rust · Facepunch Studios
Same island, pixel for pixel
A closed native world generator reimplemented from outside and graded against the live game until the difference was zero, then forked and made fast.
◆  5–20 → 60+ fps
Over the Top: WW1 · Flying Squirrel Entertainment
Every millisecond of a 200-player frame
A full per-subsystem frame budget built for a closed binary, and what it found hiding behind a healthy-looking average. Shipped in the studio's Hotfix 1.0117.
◆  crash fixed
Windrose · Kraken Express
The startup crash in the runtime
A crash that only happened to players, placed below the game in the shipped graphics runtime. Written into the studio's own FAQ, credited by name.
◐  0 → 100% spawn
theHunter: Call of the Wild · Expansive Worlds
The ghost species
A mission-reward species that appeared for some players and vanished forever for others. Root-caused in a stripped, symbol-less binary, then fixed and shipped.
◐  +79% fps
theHunter: COTW, Vurhonga Savanna · Expansive Worlds
Why one savanna tanked the frame rate
Turning "this map feels laggy" into a measured deficit, then collapsing it onto two detail tiers of one grass mesh. A data-file change, not an engine one.
◐  ~99% recovered
Stormworks: Build and Rescue · Geometa
The tick that collapsed
One save, one stale physics record, ~50 ms every tick. Fixed three independent ways, a no-op on healthy saves.
◐  crash eliminated
theHunter: Call of the Wild · Expansive Worlds
The controller that crashed the game
One specific controller crashed the game on launch, every time. Traced to an uninitialized audio object, and to a second crash the obvious fix would have caused.
◑  76% redundant
Guild Wars 2 · ArenaNet
Virtual shadow maps for a 14-year-old engine
A live per-subsystem frame budget found 76% of the shadow work recomputed identically every frame. The modern shadow path built from that finding is grafted onto the retail client from the outside, and published in progress.
